1. Executive Summary

FAMEX Model School is built on academic excellence, disciplined teaching, and a modern learning environment. This proposal outlines the design, development, and deployment of two connected digital systems that turn that standard into something visible and operational for parents, staff, and students:

  1. A professional, mobile-first school website that presents FAMEX Model School to prospective parents, regulators, and the public, and acts as the school's permanent communication hub.
  2. A Computer-Based Testing (CBT) platform — covering class tests, terminal examinations, and JAMB/WAEC/NECO-format mocks — with secure question banking, automatic and teacher-aided marking, offline/LAN capability for exam halls, and management reporting.

When delivered, the systems will:

  • Give FAMEX a credible, findable online presence that drives admissions enquiries 24 hours a day.
  • Cut per-term paper, printing, and marking overhead and remove calculation errors from result compilation.
  • Let teachers build and reuse question banks across terms — objective and theory, with partial-mark support.
  • Let students sit exams reliably even when internet is unstable, using an offline runner that saves answers locally and syncs automatically on reconnection.
  • Give proprietor and management real-time visibility into performance by class, subject, student, and question.
  • Position FAMEX students for external CBT examinations (JAMB UTME, WAEC CBT, NECO) by making computer-based sittings routine.

The systems are delivered as one integrated platform with shared login, so the website and CBT are parts of a single digital front door for the school.

2. Background and Need

Parents today begin their school search online. A school without a credible website is filtered out before the first visit; a school with outdated information or broken contact forms loses enquiries to the next school on the list. Prospective parents expect to find admissions requirements, fee guidance, recent news, photos of facilities, and a working phone or WhatsApp channel in one place — and they expect it to load cleanly on a phone.

Internally, paper-based examination administration is increasingly difficult to sustain:

  • Recurring cost: every test and exam cycle demands fresh printing, answer booklets, and collation materials.
  • Marking workload: teachers spend evenings marking scripts a computer can grade in seconds; theory scripts still need professional judgment, but a faster question-by-question interface can cut marking time dramatically.
  • Human error: transposition mistakes, lost scripts, and incorrect totals affect students and the integrity of terminal reports.
  • Limited insight: on paper, it is difficult to see which question 70% of a class failed until long after the exam.
  • External-exam readiness: JAMB, WAEC, and NECO run core papers as CBT; students who only ever write on paper enter those halls at a disadvantage.
  • Malpractice pressure: paper passes through many hands; leakage, copying, and after-exam alterations are harder to prevent without randomisation and access logs.

3. Project Objectives

The engagement succeeds when, by launch, FAMEX can point to these measurable outcomes:

  1. A responsive, SEO-ready public website, all sections populated, with an admin panel that lets designated staff update content without calling a developer.
  2. A working online admissions enquiry channel, with every enquiry delivered to a designated school email and/or WhatsApp number.
  3. A secure CBT platform deployed for at least one pilot class during the engagement — one class test, one mid-term, one full mock cycle before handover.
  4. Automatic marking for all objective questions (MCQ, true/false) with scores computed instantly and accurately.
  5. A teacher theory-marking carousel that presents essay/fill-in answers one question at a time across all scripts, with one-touch partial marks and a finalise step that totals objective + theory and publishes grades.
  6. A reusable question bank per subject, class, and topic — supporting images, tables, and accounting/mathematical notation — with bulk upload from Word/Excel templates.
  7. Management dashboards for class performance, subject performance, item analysis, and individual student reports, exportable to PDF and Excel.
  8. Offline/LAN exam capability: students can start, sit, and submit on the school network without internet; the runner resumes on restart without losing answers.
  9. Anti-malpractice configuration: shuffled questions/options, one active session per student, login & submission logs, optional LAN-only enforcement.
  10. Hands-on training for every teacher and a sample of students, with printed quick-start guides for each role.

4.2 CBT Software

Administrator (Management) module

  • Create and manage classes, arms, subjects, sessions, terms, students, and teacher accounts from one dashboard.
  • Bulk import of students/teachers from Excel/CSV templates (provided).
  • Schedule tests and exams with: start date/time, closing window, duration, shuffle rules, whether results are shown immediately or on release, and whether the exam is restricted to the school LAN.
  • Approve, publish, or retract exams before students see them.
  • View, filter, and export results by student, class, subject, term, or question.
  • Performance dashboards: class average, subject strength/weakness, top/bottom performers, pass/fail against pass-mark.
  • Item analysis: for every question, percentage of students choosing each option — identifying badly-worded questions or topics to re-teach.
  • Broadsheet generation (one class, one subject, one term) exportable to Excel and printable PDF.
  • User activity log (logins, exam starts/submissions, result views) for audit.
  • Account controls: reset passwords, disable accounts, enforce one active session per student during exams.

Teacher module

  • Build and maintain question banks organised by subject, class, and topic.
  • Question types supported:
    • Multiple choice (single-best answer), 2–6 options.
    • True/False.
    • Fill-in-the-blank / short answer (auto-matched with teacher override for partial marks).
    • Essay / theory (free-text; teacher marks via a dedicated carousel with one-touch 0 / ¼ / ½ / ¾ / full-mark chips and numeric partial-mark input).
  • Images, tables, and basic mathematical/accounting notation in questions and options (T-accounts, timelines, diagrams).
  • Shuffle question order and option order per student to reduce copying.
  • Bulk upload of questions from Word or Excel templates (provided).
  • Exam builder: pick questions by topic/difficulty, set duration and marks, preview exactly as the student sees it.
  • Live marking queue for theory: per-question progress bar, "pending only" filter, one-click finalise-and-publish when all scripts are marked.
  • Release or withhold results per exam.

Student module

  • Secure login with unique student credentials; optional PIN for exam sittings.
  • Clean exam interface: large countdown timer (red in final minutes), question navigation with answered/unanswered/flagged states, flag-for-review button.
  • Auto-save of every answer as it is typed/selected; auto-submit when time expires.
  • Resume-from-interruption: device restart, browser close, or network drop restores the student's exact position, answers, and remaining time.
  • "Your sittings" status view: available, in progress, submitted (waiting to sync if offline), submitted-and-synced, marked/released.
  • Instant result view for objective exams where the school permits (toggleable); theory results appear once the teacher finalises marking.
  • Practice mode for self-study: past-question quizzes by subject/topic with immediate feedback.
  • Result history: every past exam with question-by-question breakdown retained for revision.

Offline/LAN capability (core feature)

  • Teachers/admins can pre-download exam packages to student devices ahead of the sitting (e.g., the night before) without starting the countdown timer on the server.
  • Students can start, answer, and submit while completely offline (airplane-mode safe); answers are encrypted and stored locally.
  • On reconnection — school LAN or later at home — answers sync automatically; duplicate/replayed submissions are rejected idempotently so a student cannot submit twice.
  • Late submissions (after the scheduled window closes) are flagged for teacher/management review rather than silently accepted or dropped.
  • Failed syncs are surfaced with a one-tap Retry button, so no paper is lost.
  • Answer keys are never sent to the student device — even in the offline package — so a compromised device cannot leak the paper.

Security and integrity

  • Role-based access control: Administrator, Teacher, Student (future-proofed for Parent and Bursar).
  • One active session per student during exam windows; login and submission logs retained per sitting.
  • Optional LAN-only enforcement: an exam can be set so it is reachable only from the school network during sitting hours.
  • Encrypted password storage; CSRF and common web-attack protections.
  • No answer keys on the client; all objective scoring runs server-side at submission.
  • Automated database backups (daily retained 30 days; weekly retained longer).

Reporting

  • Individual result slips (name, class, subject, score, percentage, grade, remark, teacher comment).
  • Class broadsheets (students × questions or students × subjects).
  • Item analysis: facility index and discrimination index per question.
  • Termly/annual performance summaries by class and subject.
  • Export to Excel (.xlsx) and printable PDF.

5. Proposed Approach and Technology

The platform is built with modern, widely supported web technologies chosen for reliability, low maintenance cost, and ease of handover. The stack is deliberately standard — not tied to any one developer — so future developers (including internal IT staff as the school grows) can extend it.

Deployment modes (advised on in Phase 1):

  • Online (cloud-hosted): best for the public website and for practice tests/homework quizzes from home.
  • Offline/LAN: same platform installed on a school server or dedicated lab machine, accessed over the local network; exams run without public internet, results sync to the cloud when connectivity returns.
  • Hybrid (recommended): website online; CBT examinations on the LAN during sittings, syncing to the cloud dashboard for management reporting.

Design principles: mobile-first; accessible contrast and labelling; teacher-first marking flow (question-by-question across all scripts); student-first exam UX (large buttons, no intrusive animations, no surprises on auto-submit); fail-safe networking; no data lock-in — the school owns its data and exports are always available.

10. Benefits to the School

To the Proprietor and Management

  • A professional online identity that builds trust with parents and supports admission drives.
  • Direct visibility into academic performance — by class, subject, and question — without waiting for paper collation.
  • A defensible audit trail: every sitting, answer, mark, and login is logged and retrievable.
  • A platform that grows with the school; future phases (parent portal, online fees, attendance) extend the same system rather than replacing it.
  • Reduced reliance on any single staff member's personal spreadsheet or paper record.

To Teachers

  • Dramatically reduced marking load for objective papers — scripts graded at submission time.
  • Faster, more consistent theory marking: one question at a time across every script, with partial-mark shortcuts and auto-totalling on finalise.
  • Reusable question bank — a good question set once and reused across terms/mocks with shuffle.
  • Bulk upload from Word/Excel means teachers do not retype existing questions.
  • Item analysis highlights questions that are too easy, too hard, or ambiguous — improving future teaching and question setting.

To Students

  • Early, repeated CBT exposure reduces anxiety and builds competence for JAMB, WAEC, and NECO.
  • Fairer sittings: randomised questions/options reduce copying; auto-submit removes time disputes.
  • No lost answers: auto-save and resume behaviour means a power cut or crash does not cost a student their paper.
  • Faster feedback: objective results immediately where the school allows; theory results sooner because marking is faster.
  • Practice mode supports self-directed revision using questions the school has already set.

To Parents

  • A website they can share with relatives and that gives them confidence in their school choice.
  • Clear public information on admissions, calendar, and contact channels — fewer routine calls to the front desk.
  • (Future phase) Parent-facing portal for results and announcements, built on the same platform.

Operationally

  • Elimination of per-exam printing, answer-booklet, and collation costs, which recur every term across every class.
  • Reduced staff overtime spent on manual marking and result compilation.
  • Lower risk of result errors that damage reputation and require costly re-issuance.
  • A platform paid for once (plus modest recurring hosting) rather than a per-student, per-term SaaS fee that grows with the school.

12. About the Developer

Adewuyi Adeyemi Reuben is an educator with a Higher National Diploma (HND) in Accounting and direct classroom experience teaching Financial Accounting, Economics, Government, Civic Education, and Business Studies across JSS 1 to SS 3. That teaching background is central to this proposal: the CBT is designed around how teachers actually set questions (subject-by-subject, topic-by-topic, with images and tabular material for accounts and economics) and how students actually experience an exam hall (anxiety, the need for a clear timer, the consequences of a device dying mid-paper).

The design priorities — offline reliability, one-question-at-a-time theory marking, resume-after-interruption, no answer keys on student devices, item analysis for teachers — all come from classroom observation of what goes wrong during paper exams and what makes teachers resist or embrace a digital system. The website is designed with the same practical bias: parents find what they need in under a minute; school staff update news and gallery photos without calling a developer; enquiries actually reach someone responsible.

References from schools currently using the platform (or earlier deployments) are available on request.
